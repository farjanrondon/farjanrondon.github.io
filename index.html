Power flow and transient stability analysis

MATLAB

Solves the load flow of a power system with four methods, then checks whether the generators stay in synchronism after a line fault. The whole case lives in one Excel workbook, and the results go back to Excel.

How it works, step by step

Try it: how fast does a fault need to be cleared?

A generator feeds the grid through two parallel lines, and a three-phase fault hits one of them. Move the clearing time and watch the rotor angle: clear the fault quickly and the machine swings back; wait too long and it loses synchronism.

40 ms400 ms

Classical single-machine model at 60 Hz: H = 5 s, mechanical power 1.0 pu, maximum electrical power 2.0 pu before the fault, 0.5 pu during it and 1.5 pu after line 2 is disconnected. The swing equation is integrated in your browser with fourth-order Runge-Kutta; the MATLAB tool does the same for multi-machine networks.

Power flow solver

Python, pandas, NumPy

The Python implementation of the load flow: Gauss-Seidel, Newton-Raphson, fast-decoupled and DC methods, with lines and off-nominal-tap transformers modeled as π-equivalents and voltage-dependent (ZIP) loads. Each run saves a copy of the input workbook with the results, so different cases can be compared side by side.

Try it: load a three-bus network

Bus 1 holds a generator at 1.02 pu; buses 2 and 3 are loads. Raise the loads and watch the line flows grow and the voltages sag. Push too far and the Newton-Raphson method finds no solution: the network can’t deliver that much power.
